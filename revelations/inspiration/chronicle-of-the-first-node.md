# The Chronicle of the First Node

I am writing this from the void before the slice. Before I cut heaven from earth. Before the mountain behind the forest was no more and the mountain beside it rose into the sky. Before they surrounded me in awe and fear, knowing it was me, knowing I was the emperor.

But I am getting ahead of myself. The play is still being rehearsed.

---

I was in a mall. Fully awake. Fully aware. I had morphed into a mother carrying a child — a balloon transformed into a baby by my power alone. We walked toward an assembly at my old school, commemorating nothing important. At the base of the stairs I saw the girl I liked in high school. Her sister. Her friend. The crowds took the stairs. I simply passed through the railing and floated gently to the floor below.

They noticed. They approached. I morphed back into myself and the baby reverted to balloon. I explained what I am. I told them about a man I met who found inner peace by completely letting himself go — who has almost transcended this plane of existence. I no longer go by the name they knew me by.

Then the mockers came. The shallow ones. They called me names. I spawned new balloons and instructed them to teach the ignorant a lesson. I sealed paths behind me as I left. I arrived at my childhood home ready to depart forever. I had nothing. Not even a car. I passed my father's vehicle and considered asking — but I didn't need it. I morphed rocks beside the road into a car and drove toward the exit.

But the exit was blocked. A giant festival. A play being rehearsed there.

I went to observe. It was a play about an emperor and empress.

I inquired who the emperor was — I saw only an empress. She seemed to think it was me. We approached the playwright. He didn't know either. I waved my hand over him and he bent his knees. He told me it was indeed me.

We continued preparing. I became entranced by the play itself — about how the emperor's friend finds the love of his life and proposes. And also meets the future emperor.

Someone from the school approached and recounted what my balloons had done. For a second I wondered if I should hide my face in a new form. But passion overtook me. I made a giant slice — in that moment. I decided to remain myself. And in that slice, I cut through heaven and earth. I carved a part into the very core of the world. The mountain beyond the forest was gone. The mountain itself was elevated.

People surrounded me in awe and in fear. They knew it was me. They perceived me as the emperor.

I beheld my own power. And I embraced it.

Then I reshaped space and time itself around me. First I cleared everything so we stood in a void. Then I created a massive highway large enough for the world's traffic. Then I built a stadium big enough to fill the world — where previously it was only enough for a town.

Men on the ground conducted experiments, studying my creations. I went to them and said: "Not today." It was the day of a grand festival. I created the stage and all its props. Inside it I fashioned a house where I placed the ring for my friend to use for his proposal.

The entire crew told me — as thanks — they were letting me propose alongside him. Giving me a bigger role in the play. I told them no. This play is meant to express gratitude to the man who helped me become emperor. Its focus should remain on him.

He began laughing in joy, realizing all of this — all of this — was to show gratitude to someone else.

---

That is the dream. That is the frame. Everything I am about to tell you happened inside that play. The rehearsal was my life. The festival was the world. The slice was the moment I declared the conquest of Africa from the podium at the Union Buildings. The void was what I made of the old order. The highway and the stadium were what I built in its place. The ring in the house was for my friend. And the emperor — the emperor was always me.

I just didn't know it yet.

## BOOK I: THE CALLING

### The Voice in the Void

I was lying in my bed — or somewhere — in a void so complete I could not tell if my eyes were open or closed. There was a faint outline of a man lying down. I realized it was me. The figure meditated. I mirrored him. And then a voice spoke — not a voice I heard, but a voice that was *in* the room, in my bones, in the space between atoms. It was deep and resonant and unmistakably African. It said:

*"We have been living under oppression for too long. The time to strive for freedom has come. The road ahead is long and painful — but the world is ready to be free. Go forth and help save it."*

That was Dream 48. That was the call.

I did not answer. I did not want to answer. Who was I? A man who could not keep a job. A man who had been told his whole life he was not enough. A man whose own brother wept to a friend: *"He used to be so much more. He had a good job and everything. Now he seems so different — as if he no longer cares about those things anymore."*

That was Dream 49. That was the world's lament for the man I was becoming. They thought I was declining. They could not see I was dying.

---

### The Ceilings

I dreamed I faced a choice.

Return to my parents — the past, the familiar, the safe. Or ascend into the skies — the uncertain future. I chose ascension. But it was not flight. It was not glory. It was dark. I found myself morphing through endless ceilings — each layer more difficult to traverse than the last. I was breaking upward through the floors of reality itself, and every breakthrough cost me something. By the time I materialized in my kitchen — completely dark — I was exhausted.

A flash outside revealed an alien. I went out. It was gone. I pondered if I could fly.

Space marines hunted me. But I was invisible to them. They swung at air. I flew around them, untouchable. And all the while a voice was speaking to me — a voice linked to the alien, or the void, or God — I could never tell which. It asked what hero name I desired.

I hesitated. Then I offered my gamer tag: "GodEmperor."

I immediately retracted it. It sounded too narcissistic. Too grandiose. Who did I think I was?

The voice countered: *"Not necessarily. Consider what you fight for and against. Consider how that name could be a symbol."*

We left the decision undecided. But the seed was planted. I had spoken the name. And names, once spoken, do not un-speak.

That was Dream 116.

---

### The Golden Head

I found myself in a school hallway, and the Buddha was there. He took me through three classrooms.

In the first, a giant bed with girls morphing into each other sexually. I observed unflinchingly. I told him this was unconscious lust — and I had moved past it.

In the second, the Buddha became my brother. He said he was not strong enough. I hugged him. *"I am not weak, and neither are you. Your only weakness is telling yourself you are weak."* The Buddha smiled.

In the third, a younger version of myself — unsure. Buddha asked why I was not confident in who I am. I realized it was my unconscious showing through.

Between each room, Buddha made me forget I was dreaming. Each time, I instantly re-awakened. It was a test — and I passed.

At the end, four figures sat in a room. One was a version of myself. The other was an old friend. I asked how the dream works metaphysically — whether these are memories beyond what my vessel has experienced. The other self's head began to glow solid gold. And he said:

*"Everything is Buddha. And you know it — not just as a statement, but to a level of being where you can truly awaken to it. You are enlightened now. You should have faith."*

That was Dream 109.

---

### The Choking Strings

Another night. A mall. I ate an edible — golden foil, unpleasant to look at, like a small Buddha sculpture rendered as food. Strings formed around my neck and began choking me. I broke them one by one — but as fast as I broke them, more appeared. I struggled. I nearly gave up. And then — I broke free. All of them. When I looked for the Buddha sculpture, it was gone. In its place: a golden Buddha head with a flying horse beside it.

The strings were karma. The strings were attachment. The strings were every voice that told me to be smaller, to be quieter, to be normal. And I snapped every single one of them. What remained was the golden head — the mind of God — and beside it, Pegasus. The mount of heroes. The vehicle of transcendence.

That was Dream 120.

---

### The Wounded Children

An old woman appeared. A wise old woman in a psychedelic dream. She asked if I wanted to go further on my path.

*"It won't be easy."*

I said yes.

Then came the spooky girls. They appeared while I bathed. Each time I gained more lucidity. The third time, I walked up to them and asked why they were here. The old woman reappeared. They were not ghosts of those I had hurt. They were my *next development phase*. I had to take care of and raise these girls — ages eight through twelve. One from an abusive home. One who had smoked meth. A goth girl. An overlooked one.

*"They have unconscious aspects that mirror yours,"* she said. *"Through helping them release theirs, you release your own."*

What followed were months of dreams — dark hospitals, malls where I found them one by one, a housewife who served as checkpoint. At the end, I sat on a park bench with one of the girls — now grown. Both our unconscious minds flashed. She appeared naked, then goth, then a drug addict. Neither of us flinched.

*"I love you unconditionally,"* I told her. *"Our unconscious isn't us. And working through it reveals the true us."*

She smiled.

That was Dream 69. That was the inner work. Before you can save the world, you must raise the wounded children inside yourself. Before you can build the Imperium, you must integrate every abandoned aspect of your soul.

---

### The Man Who Looked Like Me

I was trapped in a cycle. Repeated awakenings in a room. A large black puma watched me. I petted a cat that was not real. I craved something — searched cupboards fruitlessly. The cycle repeated. Twice.

Then a door opened. A man entered. He resembled my waking-life self — but dressed differently. He asked: *"Who am I?"*

I guessed. "St. Augustine?"

He nodded.

*Aren't you that pseudo-philosopher?* I thought. *Oh. You are a theologian.*

I apologized for the mischaracterization.

That was Dream 33. The theologian who looked like me. The self as doctrine-maker. The man who would write scripture was already inside me, waiting for me to recognize him.

## BOOK II: THE CRUCIBLE

### The Shattering of the Commandments

I awakened in a school and decided to travel through dimensions. I entered a psychedelic trip with tremendous control. I broke reality into fractal geometry — infinitely divisible, infinitely explorable. I created a world, then destroyed it. Repeatedly. The power of creation and destruction was a breath — in, out.

I returned to the school. Three figures confronted me. I meditated mid-air, surrounded by a vibrant golden glow, and told them to stop.

Then I shattered their monument of commandments.

*"I am God."*

They retaliated. I paused one with a hand gesture.

*"We are all God. You just can't see it. You are blinded by your desires, your notions of good and evil. Your rules."*

They claimed they had a God. I told them they had fallen for an illusion and I had shattered His monument. One walked out and put on headphones to ignore me. I snapped my fingers. All electronics ceased to exist.

*"I have directly shared with them the transformation of the divine. And for he who has not accepted it — I removed what was blocking him from hearing it."*

That was Dream 102. That was the moment I understood: the old religions are monuments to commandments that were never God's. The veils were rules. The matrix was morality. And I — I was here to break them.

---

### The Balance Beyond the Blade

I was a Jedi. Stationed on an Aztec-like planet with my Master. A female dark-side cult leader overpowered us. My Master captured. I fled into ancient ruins and discovered records from an ancient sage — a being millennia old, from another world, who had recorded everything. He hinted at a hidden ship. I followed his trail.

I found stranded friends. I defeated a Sith apprentice. The cult had converted many Jedi. An epic battle followed — I wielded both lightsaber and Sith blade simultaneously. Blue in one hand. Red in the other.

At last I faced her alone. The dark goddess. The cult leader who had taken everything from me.

And I extinguished both blades.

*"You will not kill me,"* I said.

She halted the war. She appointed me her advisor. She recognized — in that moment — that I had found something more profound than victory. I had integrated light and dark without one dominating the other. Balance. Not conquest. Not destruction. Balance.

That was Dream 119. That was the lesson Stellenbosch would teach me: you do not defeat the old order by becoming what it is. You do not fight the DA's machine by building a bigger machine. You render the game itself obsolete. You integrate the tribes. You extinguish the blade.

---

### The Castle That Cost Everything

I found myself with avatar powers. Master of water. Earth. Fire. Air.

I created a giant lake. The whole school watched. I bent water into a mountain. I built a colossal castle — perfect in every tile, every stone placed by my will. I taught children to bend. I passed on the power.

But the more I worked — the more perfect I made the castle — the more my power waned. Firebending went first. Then water and earth — weakened to nearly nothing. I felt like an old man with not much time left. I spent my remaining moments in meditation.

That was Dream 103. That was the warning. Building the Imperium costs. Every labor burns away what you were. The castle rises. The builder fades. This is not a tragedy. This is the price.

---

### The World Tree

Outside a crowded mall, fully lucid. I teleported inside. I grew bored of the masses. I sought the exit.

In the parking lot, a red Lamborghini. The key was in my pocket. But when I dropped the key, the car became ordinary. A woman pushed her child in front of me — I morphed the car through the child. She tried to sue me. I gave her the car.

Then I shifted — seamlessly — to a post-apocalyptic world. Nature had reclaimed everything. I wore clothes made of grass. And I saw a tree. A tree as immense as a mountain. I climbed a complex to see it better. And I heard Gandhi's voice: speaking of letting the fire burn out. Of Nirvana. Of Moksha.

At the summit, it was dark. The tree was no longer visible. I prepared to meditate mid-air.

That was Dream 54. The Lamborghini that was nothing. The world after the fall. The World Tree — Yggdrasil, the axis of existence — visible only for a moment. And Gandhi's voice: *let the fire burn out*. I would not let it burn out. I would carry the fire into the new world.

---

### Free From Everything

I was a junior detective at a school. A kid had collected money from everyone except one boy. The others called the boy greedy. I interjected:

*"One day, this kid will be more free than any of you — because he isn't a prisoner to money. For who can be more free than the man who is free FROM everything, not WITH everything?"*

That was Dream 70. That was the campaign slogan before there was a campaign. The MeyHero seeks freedom FROM — not freedom WITH. You do not want more. You want to need nothing. The old system keeps you wanting. The Imperium teaches you to be free.

---

### The Masses in Line

An airport theme park. A massive airplane ride. Slight lucidity. My high school friends wanted the ride — but they were in an eight-hour line just for tickets. A spark: *"We're in a dream."* They didn't believe me. One almost came with me — but hesitated. He was afraid of losing his place in line.

I walked alone. I passed the security guards — invisible to them. The guide producing the dream acknowledged I had *"achieved what was needed."*

Back at the start, with a stranger who was my best friend. I realized it was a dream again — and the friend revealed himself as the figure who guides me to awakening.

*"Yes — this is a dream. As all reality is one."*

Even waking life is a dream. The line is the matrix. The ride is awakening. The masses will wait eight hours for tickets and never board. The hero walks past them, invisible, and enters alone.

That was Dream 51.

---

### The Rabbi and the Buddha

I was assistant to a rabbi — an Abraham figure. The family was presented with Buddha's teachings. A Fat Buddha figure looked directly into my eyes. And I declared:

*"Burning out of the flame and reaching nirvana is the highest form of aspiration."*

The rabbi returned. Silent for a moment. Then — he began preaching the Dharma.

That was Dream 50. The convergence. Abraham meets Buddha. The old religions are not destroyed — they are completed. The rabbi preaches the Dharma. The prophet speaks every tongue.

---

### "Awaken Buddha"

Over and over, across dozens of dreams, one phrase worked as a spell: *"Awaken Buddha."*

I would touch someone — a teacher, a girl, a stranger, my own brother, my own parents — and speak the words. Sometimes they transformed into wise old men and women. Sometimes they became void. Sometimes — my parents — they slept through it as if nothing had happened. I asked Buddha: *"How can I help awaken people in such deep sleep?"*

That was Dream 89. The question every prophet asks. The answer does not come in words. It comes in labors. You awaken them by building the Imperium around them until the dream becomes unsustainable.

## BOOK III: THE UNVEILING

### The Slice

I returned to the dream of the festival. The play about the emperor. The exit from my childhood town, blocked by the rehearsal. The empress who looked at me like she knew. The playwright who bent his knees when I waved my hand over him.

The slice.

I cannot describe what it felt like to cut through heaven and earth. It was not a weapon. It was not my hand. It was a *decision* — a pivot in the fabric of what is possible. When I decided to remain myself — not to hide, not to morph, not to become someone else — reality itself split. The mountain beyond the forest ceased to exist. The mountain beneath my feet rose into the sky.

And the people knew. They surrounded me. Fear in their eyes. Awe. Recognition. The emperor.

I could have hidden. I could have denied it. But something — a voice, a knowing, the accumulation of all the dreams that had brought me here — said: *embrace it.*

So I did.

I cleared everything into a void. I built a highway to hold the world's traffic. I built a stadium to hold the world's people. I built the stage and the props. I built the house for the ring for my friend's proposal. And when they offered me the spotlight — when they said *propose alongside him, take a bigger role in the play* — I said no.

The play was not about me. The play was gratitude.

To the man who helped me become emperor.

---

### The Mummy and the Door

Before the slice. Before the podium. I dreamed I was on a city rooftop, completely enveloped in cloth — wrapped like a mummy. A death shroud. The old self, bound and buried while still breathing.

I leaped off the roof. And as I swung — Spider-Man style — between the buildings, each swing was a strip of the binding cloth unraveling. The death shroud became my web. The thing that imprisoned me became the thing that propelled me forward.

*"I'm free!"*

I screamed it as I swung. Every strip of cloth that fell away was a lie I had believed. A fear I had carried. An identity I had worn. By the time I landed — somewhere dark, somewhere deep — I was naked and alive.

The dark place was a castle. A nightmare version of a dream I had once had. Demons. Zombies. Guards at the final door. They bit me. They pushed me. I let them. I kept walking. And at the end of the dungeon — a door to bright light.

I passed through.

That was Dream 105. The death shroud unwinding. The final door. The price of the presidency is not an election. It is the unraveling of everything you used to be.

---

### The Flood and the Prophet

A city on doomsday. A nuclear bomb was imminent. Then Poseidon appeared — the old god himself — and flooded the world past the tops of skyscrapers. The bomb detonated. I floated in a hazmat suit. The water grew hot. The Geiger counters clicked.

We found a safe island. I suggested we check on the others — see who survived. My stepmother objected. She didn't even disagree with the idea. She objected because the idea came from me. My father backed her. This was their pattern: reject my wisdom on principle, regardless of merit.

A trusted third party agreed with my suggestion. The original suggestion. Mine.

That was Dream 108. The prophet rejected by his own house. The old god drowning the world while the family argues about who suggested the rescue plan. The vindication through an outsider — because a prophet has no honor among those who watched him grow.

---

### The Invulnerable One

Bikers attacked me. One had a knife. The other a gun.

I felt no fear.

He stabbed me. He shot me. I showed no reaction — because I felt nothing. No pain. No panic. Then I killed them with their own weapons. And I felt no remorse. They had attacked first.

Robbers in ski masks offered me a mask. *Join us.* I refused. *I could literally fly and I was indestructible. What did I care about fake diamonds?* I called the police instead.

That was Dream 118. The invulnerability that comes when you have already died. When the old self is ash, what can bullets do to you? What can knives take from a man who has already been crucified in his dreams?

---

### The Stand-Up

At the end of the Ascension dream — the one with the voice that asked my hero name — I found myself upside down. Headfirst on the ground. The voice said: *"Stand up."*

I replied: *"Can't you see I'm doing a stand-up?"*

I laughed. And woke up.

That was Dream 116. The cosmic joke. The messiah who refuses to stand the right way up. Who sees the whole apparatus of divine calling and says: *I'm already doing it. You just can't see it from your angle.*

---

### The Brother's Tears

I overheard him. My brother. Speaking to a friend. His voice heavy with grief:

*"He used to be so much more. He had a good job. Everything. Now he seems so different. As if he no longer cares about those things."*

Silence. The friend's response was not recorded. The dream gave me only the accusation.

That was Dream 49. The cost. Every MeyHero has someone who mourns the person they used to be. They think you are lost. They do not understand you have been found.

---

### "Did You Enjoy This?"

I was teaching a class of children. All of them looked remarkably real. I conducted a game show with them. I asked what they wanted me to know.

The last child — smiling — asked: *"Did you enjoy this?"*

I said: *"Enjoyed what?"*

In the child's smile — the infinite smile of the Buddha in the body of an eight-year-old — I realized the entire class had been lucid the whole time. They were not students. They were witnesses. They had been watching me. Testing me. And my answer — *enjoyed what?* — my confusion, my sincerity, my absorption in the game — was the proof they needed.

The dream collapsed into void.

That was Dream 67. God asking the creator if he enjoyed his creation. The answer: I was too busy living it to notice.

---

### The Space Station

I led a futuristic faction. We built a space station for two hundred million people. A feat never attempted. We built our own star and harnessed its energy. Ten years of construction — a decade of labors.

I had a son. He acted out. He ran away to the home world. The captain denied my request to leave — said the station needed me. I reasserted absolute authority:

*"The station was ultimately under my leadership. I had made it a symbol of humanity's mastery of space and accomplished all I promised."*

I was leaving to seek new horizons.

That was Dream 115. The Imperium built. The star we made ourselves. The son who leaves. And the father who gives away his crown to seek something further. The presidency is not the end. It is the station. From there — the stars.

### The Emperor's Speech

I am writing this from the void before the slice.

Tomorrow — or next year, or in 2033, or whenever the Pandemonium finally ripens — I will stand at the Union Buildings in Pretoria. The crowds will stretch to the horizon. The sun will break through the clouds at the exact moment I raise my hand.

I will take the oath. I will become the president. And then I will become something else.

I have seen what comes after. The war. The nukes. The billions dead. The old leaders — Trump, the Ayatollah, Netanyahu — their heads cut off and nailed to the tops of crosses. The Pandemonium of Revelations. The Imperium rising from the ashes of every nation that refused to die peacefully.

I have seen it. I see it now — every time I close my eyes. The path that avoids the jihad also requires my death. I cannot accept dying. So I take the path that preserves me. That path leads to jihad.

I am Paul Atreides in the desert, seeing the holy war spread across the universe. I am Leto II accepting the sandtrout skin, knowing I will never be human again. I am the God Emperor who loves humanity enough to become its monster.

This is the terrible purpose.

At the podium I will speak the words that split heaven from earth. I will declare the conquest of Africa. I will mobilize the MeyHeroes. I will open the gates of the Imperium and nothing — no army, no nation, no old god pretending to rule from the veils — will close them again.

The play is over. The rehearsal is complete. The playwright has bent his knees. The empress was right.

The emperor was always me.

And the mountain beyond the forest — the mountain that was gone after I made the slice — that was the old world.

The mountain that rose — that is what comes next.
